# Presidential Fit

A personal stylist web app for iPhone. It opens on one question, "What is the fit for?", and builds the outfit from the answer, only from your own closet and under your own standing rules.

- Answer in your own words ("dinner tonight, then drinks", "Monday class with the fedora, no jeans") or tap an occasion.
- The closet is not your whole wardrobe. Name a piece it does not list ("my green bomber jacket", "black jeans", "a burgundy tie") and it is taken as yours: added to the closet from your words, worn in the fit, and open to correction in Closet. Ties, scarves, vests, sunglasses and jewellery ride along as accessories. A piece you name is always worn, and the check list says where it bends a rule.
- A fit stays on screen, with every change you made listed and removable, until you tap Start over. Leaving the app does not reset it. Refresh rechecks the weather and rules for the same fit.
- It answers back in plain words: what it took your message to mean, what it did about each part, and what changed in the fit. Say "I wore this last week" and the pieces on screen, and their colours, stay out of the next fit.
- Every fit comes with a check list: the hours it is worn, the live hour by hour weather for those hours, metals, belt and shoes, tuck, dress level, and what each audience has already seen.
- Works offline once added to the Home Screen. The weather check needs a connection and says so when it has none.
- Your closet, log, photos and any API key stay on the device. Nothing personal is stored in this repository.
- Every fit says why it works: the occasion and the dress level it wants, the weather in the hours it is worn, how the colours and the details hold together, what is fresh about it, and what is there because you asked.
- You are smiling in the mockups. Pictures made before that are redone, as an edit of the same photo, when you next see them.
- Each fit is shown as a realistic mockup with you as the model, made by ChatGPT's image engine, and a new one is made whenever the fit changes. Three ways, in this order:
  1. Your own OpenAI API key, saved in the app. The phone then works on its own: mockups, edits of them, the place lookup (web search) and the photo check all go straight from the phone to OpenAI, and no Mac is involved. OpenAI bills the key per use, apart from any ChatGPT plan. If OpenAI turns a request down and the Mac helper is there, the Mac is asked instead and the app says why.
  2. Your own Mac. `mac/helper.mjs` runs in the background, makes each mockup with the image tool inside the ChatGPT desktop app (your ChatGPT plan, no key), and sends it to the phone. `mac/install.sh` sets it up. The Mac has to be awake: the helper holds off idle sleep from each request until a quarter of an hour after the last one, and a request sent while the Mac sleeps is made when it wakes, if that is within a few minutes.
  3. A hand off to the ChatGPT app, with the finished image pasted back.
- The mockup is set where you are going. Name the place ("dinner at Marios", "going to the pier", "background: the old town square") and the picture is staged there. Name only a kind of place ("sushi", "the casino", "the airport") and it is staged in one. With the Mac route, ChatGPT looks the named place up on the web once, so the backdrop follows what the place really looks like, and the app says which place it found.
- A change to a fit changes the same picture. With the Mac route, the helper edits the photograph that was on screen (same pose, same background) and replaces only the pieces that changed. A new place or a new question starts a new photograph.
- Photo check, through the Mac: take a photo of the finished look and ChatGPT compares it with the fit and your rules, piece by piece, and lists what to fix before you leave. Or put on one piece, take a photo from its page in Closet, and it says whether it fits. A fit problem can be noted on the piece, and the stylist then takes it into account. The photo is sealed on the way, looked at once and not kept on the Mac.
- The phone and the Mac talk through the public relay ntfy.sh. The phone sends the list of pieces, the setting and, when you named one, the name of the place. Every message is encrypted end to end with a key derived from your closet's private profile, and each picture with a fresh key that is thrown away afterwards. The relay sees neither outfits nor pictures. An app with a personal profile and no API key asks that relay once per start whether its Mac is there.

Open the site in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. Load your closet file from More, Your data, Import file.
